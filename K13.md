K13 is thirteen steps that make Ai answers better and simpler.
This page is written the same way, so you can check it against its own rules.

## What K13 is

K13 is a short checklist. You run it every time an Ai writes a report.
The result is one answer, called the quick squeeze.

Think of a juice box. A quick squeeze and you're hit with a burst of sweet, simple goodness: easy to consume, easy to digest ... and oddly transparent.

## What it does for an answer

- It puts the outcome first, in plain words.
- It gives every number a source.
- It says how sure we are, and why.
- It shows what the answer cost, in energy and carbon.
- It keeps sentences short, so anyone can read them.

K13 does not say an answer is true. It shows the work, so a reader can judge.

### Three words that carry a rule

- MUST means you have to.
- SHOULD means do it, unless you have a good reason not to.
- MAY means you are allowed to. It is your choice.

## Scope: when K13 applies

K13 is for a REPORT: writing that stands on its own, read after the work is done.
Think of a long task summary, an audit, or a release report.
A strong model working alone should write its answers this way too.

K13 is NOT for a one-line answer, a chat reply or a tool call.
"The file exported" is already a complete answer.

## The quick squeeze

The quick squeeze is the one response style of K13.
K13 2.x called it the juice box pass. It is the same thing.

- Plain words. Say "before", not "prior to".
- One idea per sentence.
- The outcome first, then the method.
- Short, within the limits below.
- Every number has a source, or says it is derived and how.
- The confidence is stated, with what it rests on.

### The limits

A program can check these numbers. They sit a little above the longest juice box passages in the 2.x examples.

| Limit | Value | Longest in the 2.x juice box examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| longest word | 12 letters | 8 letters |
| longest sentence | 20 words | 16 words |
| average sentence | 14 words | 11.1 words |

These are ceilings, not targets. Shorter is better.
A term in backticks or double quotes is not counted. Neither is a version, a path or a link.
Code blocks, tables and evidence are data. The limits do not apply to data.
A hype word fails, such as `revolutionary`, `seamless`, `game changing`, `cutting edge` or `unlock`.

## The thirteen steps

1. Verify state. Use real sources only. Do not guess. Do not use a plausible
   default.

2. Capture the cost before drafting anything else.
   Rule carried here (cost tracked, AiEDs v2): every Ai answer records its cost.
   It goes in the shared AiEDs store, under the v2 model. The Ai logs it. A
   person never copies numbers by hand. If a tool cannot report usage, say so.
   Do not guess.

3. State the outcome first, then the method. Use a plain sentence and short
   words. Say what happened, not how hard you worked.
   Rule carried here (human readable): short sentences, plain words. Say the
   thing, then explain it. Lead with what it means for the person. Do not build
   a wall of jargon. This rule governs every writing step from here through
   step 12.

4. Flag every roadblock. Name anything blocked, skipped or partial. Say it
   plainly. Do not bury it in a paragraph.

5. Turn the cost into meaning, not raw numbers. Example: "8 grams of carbon,
   about 3 hours of one tree's work."

6. Name the owner action. Give the exact click or the exact command. Never say
   "let me know" when a real action exists.

7. Pull the next-command template. Include the model and the effort. Match the
   situation to a template in the command template library (see the reference).
   Fill it in exactly. Any agent on any model should be able to paste it and
   run it cold.

8. Check the humor. Keep it sparse and honest. Turn it off for safety, money or
   loss. The humor rules in the reference are the law here.

9. Draft the quick squeeze. Use plain words. Give one idea per sentence. Put the
   outcome first.
   Rule carried here (one pass): there is one pass, and this is it. No other
   pass is drafted, so nothing needs to be kept in agreement. Spell words
   plainly, with no number swaps. Number swaps mark Ai bot identity. K13 is a
   published standard, not a bot.

10. Cut it to the limits. No word over 12 letters. No sentence over 20 words.
    An average sentence of 14 words or fewer. No hype word.
    Rule carried here (the limits): see "The limits". Keep a technical name and
    explain it once. The facts stay at full strength. Only the sentence gets
    simpler.

11. Source every figure. A figure is a number. Each one traces to run data, or
    is marked as derived and says how.
    Rule carried here (no untraceable figure): a number you cannot trace is a
    guess wearing a decimal point. Step 1 already forbids it. A derived figure
    names the figures it came from, and the operation.

12. State the confidence. Say high, moderate or low, and say what it rests on.
    Say what was not looked at, so the reader knows how far to trust the count.
    Rule carried here (verdict and confidence): the verdict, the confidence and
    the provenance travel with the answer. A quick squeeze that gives the
    verdict and drops the confidence is not the whole answer.

13. Run the gate. No dashes. Every claim checked. Every limit met. Fix it
    before sending.
    Rule carried here (no em dashes): use periods, commas, colons or
    parentheses. This covers the answer, code comments, docs, commit messages
    and public copy. The gate fails on a single em dash. An en dash fails the
    same way. Two hyphens used as a dash fail too. They stand in for the dash,
    so they break the rule. Use a comma, a colon or a new sentence instead.
    Only the dash as punctuation is banned. A flag such as `--json` is not a
    dash. A table rule such as `| --- |` is not a dash either.

A one-line reply still runs all thirteen steps. Yes, even the humor check.
Most steps take a second.

## The machine gate

Step 13 can be checked by a machine. Run these nine checks before a report goes out.
Checks 1 to 5 read the prose. Checks 6 to 9 read the shape.

| # | Check | Passes when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Every figure has a source | each number is in the report's run data, or is declared derived |
| 2 | The confidence is stated | "confidence", then high, moderate or low, then "because" and why |
| 3 | Plain words | no word over 12 letters outside a term, and no hype word |
| 4 | Short sentences | none over 20 words, and an average of 14 or fewer |
| 5 | No dashes | no em dash, no en dash, no two-hyphen stand-in |
| 6 | The cost line is there | the AiEDs tile gives `energyKWh` and `gCO2e`, each a number or "not reported" |
| 7 | The twelve tiles are there, in order | see "The report template" |
| 8 | No evidence block lacks a result | every command has its result, and every result its command |
| 9 | "What's next" is clean | a role and an effort, or "No next step, this work is complete", and no model name |

A derived figure gets its own sentence:

```
Derived: 8 from 5 and 3 by adding them.
```

A green result means the shape and the stated sources are right.
It does not mean the answer is right. The gate cannot judge plainness beyond the limits.
It cannot tell if a claim was really checked, or if the confidence is the right one.
The reference lists what the gate cannot check, and what K13 does not claim.

## The report template

A K13 report carries these twelve tiles, in this order:

1. A header strip.
2. A one-sentence finding.
3. A verdict row, with its gate string and its confidence.
4. How it fits.
5. An environment card.
6. A checks table.
7. Evidence blocks.
8. "Discrepancies": anything that did not match.
9. What's next.
10. Owner actions.
11. What was NOT done, and why.
12. The AiEDs disclosure.

A tile with nothing to report says so in one line. It is never dropped.
The reference explains each tile. Example files are `templates/temp1ate.md` and `templates/temp1ate.html`.

## One fact, one pass

Here is one real fact written as a quick squeeze. The longest sentence has 15 words.

> Six of our copies of the rules are old. They say version 1.1.0. The new rules
> are version 3.0.0. We looked in four places, so there may be an old copy we
> did not find. Confidence is high that these six are old, because we read each
> version number. Confidence is moderate that six is the whole count, because
> we searched four places. The old copies need to be replaced with the new one.

Run data: 6 copies found old, 4 places searched, version 1.1.0 found, version
3.0.0 current.

Every number is in the run data. The confidence has its reason. No sentence or word is long. There is no dash.
The `@randomknights/k13` package tests this passage on every build.

## How to use it

1. Do the work, and keep the real results.
2. Write the answer as a quick squeeze, with the twelve tiles if it is a report.
3. Run `npx k13 check your-report.md`. Fix every finding.
4. Say what you ran. See "How to say a product conforms".

## How to say a product conforms

A product MAY offer a setting named "quick squeeze", on by default.
On shows the K13 answer. Off shows the product's own answer, and still shows the AiEDs cost line.
A report or a tool MAY state conformance in one line:

```
conforms to K13 3.0.0, quick squeeze, gate checks 1 to 9
```

Name only the checks that ran and passed. Never claim a check that did not run.
